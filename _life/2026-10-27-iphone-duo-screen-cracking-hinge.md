## What owners are reporting

The iPhone Duo — Apple's first foldable, starting at **$1,999** — went on sale on **Friday, October 23**. By the launch weekend, a consistent failure pattern was circulating on Reddit, X, and TikTok:

- Inner screens **cracking along the hinge**, with bright lines running across the display from the crease and dead pixels along the fold.
- An Australian owner reported a crack after **roughly six hours of ownership and fewer than 20 openings** — he felt something under the display near the hinge, then heard an audible crack as he kept unfolding it (reported by the New York Post and Gadgets 360).
- A Dallas owner said the hinge went unusually tight after **about four folds** during setup, then made a loud pop when pushed past the resistance (New York Post).
- Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported speaking to **a dozen owners** with the same sequence: hinge stiffens, crunching sound, crack along the centre crease, bright lines across one side of the screen.
- On October 24, one widely shared X post counted **7 photos and multiple TikTok videos** showing the identical failure.

Store demo units were also reported affected, according to Digital Trends. Some images circulating online may be AI-generated, as Technology.org noted — which makes the true scale harder to judge from social media alone.

## Apple's answer, and iFixit's theory

On **Monday, October 26**, Apple acknowledged the problem:

> "We are aware of an issue impacting the inner display on a small number of iPhone Duo units. If users experience issues with their iPhone Duo display, they should contact AppleCare for a replacement."

Apple added that affected units tend to show the fault **early**. 9to5Mac reported that an AppleCare+ plan is **not required** for a replacement. Apple has not said how many units are affected, or what is causing the failures.

Repair specialists at **iFixit** offered the most specific theory so far — explicitly a theory, not a confirmed cause. Using teardown footage, patents, and CT scans to build a 3D model of the hinge, iFixit found the reported damage clusters just below a display cable near a **central support bar**. That bar may be failing to drop out of the screen's way as the phone folds, creating a small stress hotspot exactly where screens are breaking; curved guide rails may also rise into the panel. In the same analysis, iFixit called the Duo the **most repairable foldable it has tested**.

The counter-data point: CNET's test unit had passed **more than 55,000 folds** without the issue, and Engadget's review unit was unaffected. This does not look like every hinge — it looks like some hinges.

## Why the replacement is the hard part

A free replacement solves the defect. It does not solve the queue.

This is a phone that sold out in minutes in Seoul and Delhi on launch day, with online delivery already pushed to **January 25, 2027** (see our launch-day report). The Dallas owner above was warned by AT&T that a carrier replacement could send him to the **back of that January queue**. Another owner was offered an express replacement only with a hold for the phone's **full price**. Owners who walked into an Apple Store fared better — at least one was swapped a new unit on the spot — but that depends on local stock that barely exists.

And if a screen fails outside this programme later, the stakes are not small: Technology.org put the out-of-warranty inner-screen repair at **$999** — half the price of the phone.

## The verdict

Read the pattern, not the photos. A design flaw that breaks every unit eventually would show up across the fold counts — in the 55,000-fold test units, in week-two units, everywhere. What is documented so far is the opposite signature: a minority of units failing **within hours and within their first handful of folds**, in the same place, with the same sound. That is a batch / assembly defect profile, and Apple's "shows up early" framing matches it.

That makes the practical advice simple. If you own a Duo, fold it normally and often this week — a bad hinge is better found inside the replacement window than in month three. If yours has already survived hundreds of folds, the evidence so far says you are probably clear. If you are still waiting for a January delivery, this is arguably good news arriving before your phone does.

What would change the verdict is a number Apple has not given: the affected share. "Small number" covers both 0.1% and 5%, and at $1,999 — with a $999 screen behind it — buyers are entitled to know which end of that range they are in. Until Apple publishes it, the cracking story is a contained launch defect with an unusually expensive tail risk, not a foldable failure. But contained is a claim that needs a denominator.
