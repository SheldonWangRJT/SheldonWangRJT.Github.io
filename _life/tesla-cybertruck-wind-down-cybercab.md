## What Musk announced

Elon Musk announced the end of the Cybertruck in a single post on X in the early hours of Friday, November 6, 2026: "Tesla will be winding down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production."

The truck's lead engineer, Wes Morrill, confirmed the tone a few minutes later: "Cybertruck was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab." Musk, replying to a user who called the Cybertruck the best vehicle Tesla has ever made, wrote: "True, but the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time."

What Musk did not say is whether Cybertruck production moves to another factory or stops altogether. Reuters and CNN both note that Gigafactory Texas is the only known Tesla plant equipped to build the truck's specialized stainless-steel body, so a move would require retooling somewhere else — and Tesla has announced no such plan.

## The numbers Musk did not mention

Musk framed the wind-down as a capacity decision. The sales data frames it as a demand decision, and the two frames are not hard to reconcile.

Cox Automotive estimates Tesla sold 4,323 Cybertrucks in the US in the third quarter of 2026, bringing the total for the first nine months of the year to 11,586 — down 28% year over year. Tesla does not report Cybertruck deliveries separately; it lumps the truck into an "other models" category with the Model S, Model X and Semi, which delivered 8,295 vehicles in Q3, down 48% year over year. Electrek estimates roughly 7,000 to 8,000 of those were Cybertrucks — a higher figure than Cox's, but the same story in either count: the truck is selling at roughly one-tenth of the 250,000 vehicles a year Musk originally said Tesla hoped to sell.

The truck has also had 11 US safety recalls as of November 6, 2026, including software fixes delivered over the air, according to TechSpot. Deliveries began in late 2023, so the production run will have lasted under three years — a short life for a vehicle unveiled in 2019 as the future of the American pickup.

## The bet underneath it

The Cybercab is the other side of the trade. It is a two-seat vehicle with butterfly doors and no steering wheel, pedals or mirrors, described by Tesla as a "purpose-built fully autonomous vehicle." According to Tesla's second-quarter 2026 update, production of the Cybercab had already begun at the same Texas plant. Musk has said the Cybercab will eventually be built in greater numbers than all of Tesla's other vehicles combined, and Tesla's website now has a form for buyers interested in Cybercabs for commercial or fleet use. There is still no date for a private customer to buy one.

The context is a lineup being narrowed around autonomy. Production of the Model S and Model X ended in April 2026, and now the Cybertruck line is being handed to the Cybercab. Tesla shares fell 10.8% on Thursday — before the announcement, as part of the broader market selloff — and were up about 2% in premarket trading on Friday, according to Investopedia.

## The judgment

Both of Musk's stories can be true at once: the line is being handed over because the Cybercab needs the space, and the Cybercab needs the space partly because the Cybertruck never filled it. The honest read is that Tesla is killing its most recognizable vehicle after under three years of deliveries to bet the factory on a car with no steering wheel that a private customer cannot yet buy.

If the robotaxi bet pays off, nobody will remember the Cybertruck as anything but a prototype that shipped. If it does not, Tesla will have traded a real — if small — product line for a promise. The number to watch is not Cybertruck sales anymore; it is how many Cybercabs actually roll off that Texas line next year, and whether anyone outside a fleet contract is allowed to buy one.
