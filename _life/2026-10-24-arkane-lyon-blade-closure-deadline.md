*Status discipline: Le Monde's reporting, union statements, Microsoft's statements, and later same-day updates are labeled separately below, as of October 23–24, 2026. No closure and no cancellation of Marvel's Blade has been announced. A consultation process is not a final decision — Microsoft says so, and French law is built on that distinction.*

## The report, and the same-day update

On **October 23, 2026**, France's *Le Monde* reported the hard version of a story that had been vague since July: **Arkane Lyon** — the 27-year-old studio behind *Arx Fatalis* (2002), the *Dishonored* games, and *Deathloop* — faces a **November 13** deadline to find a buyer, with **217 jobs** at risk, under a consultation that unions described as going **"badly."**

Hours later the deadline moved. Microsoft told *PC Gamer* it had **extended the consultation period by one additional month**, pushing the buyer search past November 13, while exploring options that include finding a partner to support the studio through the development work still ahead. Microsoft also stressed that the July documents describing a possible closure are **part of the required consultation process and do not represent a final decision**.

Both halves matter. Without the first, there is no urgency; without the second, there is no accuracy. What exists today is a live sale process with a clock — not a shutdown notice.

## How it got here

- **July 16:** Arkane management told employees — ten days after Xbox's wider layoffs were announced — that Xbox planned to close the studio, according to reporting on the July documents. A July agreement includes a job-protection plan describing a permanent end to the company's activities, alongside the buyer-search consultation.
- **July, formally:** Microsoft placed Arkane into consultation with its Works Council (Social and Economic Committee) to review strategic options. That announcement set no buyer deadline and announced no closure. Xbox also said at the time that **no announced first-party game had been cancelled** in the restructuring.
- **The law:** France's **Florange Law** requires a company in Microsoft's position to spend **at least three months searching for a buyer** before it can close the site. The consultation runs under Article L.1233-57-9 of the French Labor Code, covering a takeover of all or part of the business. Reporting describes "several forms of takeover" under consideration; union fallback options discussed include re-employing staff at other Xbox studios — or taking Microsoft to court.
- **If no buyer is found:** the proposed closure could begin at the **end of 2026 and run into early 2027**, per the consultation documents as reported.

The unions have asked for more time — and, with the one-month extension, have partially received it. A union delegate told *Le Monde* that negotiations over the employee protection plan were going badly and that representatives were not being given a real choice. That is the union's characterization; Microsoft's public line is that it keeps the committee and employees informed and is **"actively seeking a partner to support the next phase of development and enable the project to be brought to market."**

## The game in the middle of it

The project is **Marvel's Blade** — a mature, single-player, third-person game set in a quarantined section of Paris, announced in **December 2023** and shown since only in concept art. Two reported claims now sit in tension:

- A union representative said Blade is **nearing the end of production** — in final polishing and optimization — and called leaving it unreleased a monumental waste of the team's work.
- IGN has reported development was on track for a **new reveal in early 2027 and a Q4 2027 release** — a timeline that does not read as "weeks from done."

Both can be partly true (content-complete is not ship-ready), but they cannot both be the whole truth, and neither is an announcement. Microsoft has announced **no release date and no cancellation**. Bloomberg's Jason Schreier corroborated that "multiple paths" are being explored and said he would be surprised if the studio is shut down — informed reporting, still not a decision.

Context makes the stakes plain. Arkane Lyon's departure from the Xbox portfolio was announced alongside **Double Fine, Compulsion Games, Undead Labs, and Ninja Theory**; most of those studios chose independence again, while Ninja Theory is now expected to shut down after an outside-investor deal fell through. And Lyon has already watched the American half of its own name disappear: **Arkane Austin**, founded in 2006, was closed in **2024** after the poorly received *Redfall*. Lyon is what remains of Arkane.

## My call

The instructive contrast is Austin versus Lyon. The same parent, the same restructuring logic, two legal universes: in Texas, a studio ends with an announcement; in France, it ends — if it ends — after a state-mandated auction of itself, in public, with unions, deadlines, and extensions. Whatever one thinks of the Florange Law, it has done the one thing severance alone never does: it has **converted a closure into a market test**. If Blade is genuinely near-finished and the team that made *Dishonored* is genuinely intact, a buyer or partner should exist at some price — and the process now has roughly an extra month to find out whether that sentence is true.

That is also why July's Xbox assurance — no announced first-party game cancelled — is now load-bearing. Blade has not been cancelled; it has been **attached to a studio that is for sale**. Those are different states, but they converge if the clock runs out: a finished-enough Marvel game with no studio to ship it is a cancellation by logistics rather than by memo. Watch the extended deadline, not the statements. A named partner ends this story well. Silence through the extension ends it the other way.
