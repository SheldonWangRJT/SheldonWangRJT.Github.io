*All figures are as reported October 28, 2026 by GE Vernova's own release, Reuters, Barron's, Investor's Business Daily and MarketWatch coverage of Vertiv. Share-price moves are intraday snapshots from that coverage, not closing prices. This is analysis, not investment advice.*

## Two prints, one morning, one bottleneck

**GE Vernova** — the power business spun out of GE — reported:

- **Revenue $12.64 billion, up 27%**, versus about $12 billion expected (Barron's / company release).
- **EPS $4.81, up 193% year over year**, versus $4.01 expected; adjusted EBITDA $1.9 billion versus $1.7 billion expected.
- **Orders $29.3 billion, up 96%** — BofA had expected about $21 billion. Power-segment orders were $21.8 billion, **up 178%**.
- **Backlog $197 billion**, up $20 billion in one quarter, with management expecting it to exceed $200 billion in Q4 and at least **130 GW** of gas equipment under contract by year-end.
- **Guidance raised for the third time this year**: 2026 revenue now about $46.5–47.5 billion.
- And a **$9 billion acquisition of Trench Group**, the high-voltage grid-equipment maker, at about 18× expected 2026 EBITDA per Barron's, adding roughly $5 billion of backlog and a Charlotte plant set to make 765-kV equipment from late 2027 (Reuters).

**Vertiv**, which sells power and cooling inside the data centre, reported the same morning: **sales $4.31 billion, up 61%**, versus $3.75–3.76 billion expected; **adjusted EPS $2.21 versus $1.82 expected**; **organic orders more than doubled**; full-year sales guidance raised to **$14.45–14.85 billion**; shares surged roughly 16–17% premarket, per MarketWatch. CEO Giordano Albertazzi's line — "We are in the middle of the largest infrastructure build-out and it's accelerating" — was the day's most-quoted sentence for a reason.

## Why the winner fell

GE Vernova did all that and its shares still whipsawed — up in premarket, then **down about 5.7%** at one point Wednesday morning, per Barron's and Investor's Business Daily. The reason is the most useful number in either report: **new gas-equipment contracts of 11 GW were down 45% versus Q2, and new slot reservation agreements — 8 GW of that total — were down 56% sequentially.**

Slots matter because they are the industry's deposit system. A slot reservation is not a binding order; it is a paid place in the turbine queue. The +178% Power order headline, GLJ Research's Gordon Johnson argued, is mostly *existing slots converting* into orders, while the inflow of *new* slots "missed by a mile." Bulls have Bernstein's read — the print "strengthens the case for a durable earnings upcycle" — and a management point worth weighing: Q3 demand came from about 100 customers in 25 countries, roughly 80% traditional utilities and industrials rather than data centres, and gas order prices per kilowatt are expected to rise about 50% for full-year 2026 versus 2025. Bears have the slot line and an implied weak Q4 free-cash-flow guide.

Both can be true at once. That is what a bottleneck looks like in its mature phase: today's revenue is yesterday's panic, booked. The argument is no longer whether power demand is real — $197 billion of backlog settles that — but whether the *rate of new panic* peaked in Q2.

## The judgment

For two years the AI trade asked who sells the chips. October 28 is the day the answer visibly rotated: the companies with cash coming in *today*, backlog measured in hundreds of billions, and pricing power of +50% per kilowatt are the ones selling turbines, transformers, switchgear and cooling — while a chip-adjacent name like Meta, in the background of this same earnings season, just posted its first negative free cash flow since its IPO on AI spending.

My read: **trust the backlog, watch the slots.** Vertiv's doubling orders and GE Vernova's backlog say the build-out is still accelerating in physical terms — you cannot fake a transformer order book for long. But slot reservations are the canary management itself created, and a 56% sequential drop deserves more respect than a headline +96%. If Q4 slots recover, October 28 was a buying-the-whipsaw day. If they fall again, this print will be remembered as the moment the power bottleneck stopped tightening — long before it loosened.

Either way, the centre of gravity has moved. The scarcest asset in AI is no longer a GPU. It is a gigawatt, delivered on time.
