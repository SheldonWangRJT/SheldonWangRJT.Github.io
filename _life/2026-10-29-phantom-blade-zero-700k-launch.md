## A launch day, in numbers

The climb is the story, because each snapshot was reported as "the" number by a different outlet at a different hour:

| Launch-day snapshot | Steam concurrent players |
| --- | --- |
| Within ~1 hour of launch | ~400,000 |
| ~3 hours in | 431,541 |
| 7:25 AM CT Thursday | 650,054 — No. 3 on Steam, 581 players behind PUBG, ahead of Dota 2 |
| Later Thursday (SteamDB, via launch-day coverage) | **705,643 peak** |

Context makes the scale legible. The three games ahead of or around it at mid-morning — Counter-Strike 2 (~1.01 million), PUBG (~650,635), Dota 2 (~583,084) — are free-to-play fixtures years old. Phantom Blade Zero is a $59.99 paid single-player game less than a day old, and it was simultaneously Steam's No. 1 top seller by revenue, with a Twitch peak above 102,000 viewers. Pre-launch demand was visible too: Alinea Analytics estimated roughly 850,000 digital pre-sales worth about $43 million (other coverage rounds the pre-launch total past 1 million copies including PS5 and WeGame), with about 62.5% of pre-sales from Greater China and over 3 million wishlists across platforms.

Critical reception backed the commercial surge rather than merely preceding it. Metacritic sits at **88** (OpenCritic 90, per launch coverage), reviewers praising combat, boss design, and more than 30 weapon types, while docking the story. Steam user reviews started at 86% positive on a small early sample and settled to "Very Positive" at about 81% across 10,000+ reviews as the day wore on — the normal pattern of a launch-day sample growing up, not a collapse.

## The argument this launch settles — and the one it does not

What it settles: Black Myth: Wukong was not a one-off. A second Chinese-developed, Unreal Engine 5, single-player action game — this one a "Kung Fu Punk" wuxia world where you play Soul, an assassin with 66 days to live — has now opened at a scale most Western AAA launches never reach. The pipeline reading (Chinese studios, global Steam day-one, console parity on PS5) is now two data points, not one, and that is what publishers will take into their 2027 greenlight meetings.

What it does not settle: whether this is a Wukong-class event. Wukong's peak was over **2.4 million** concurrent players — more than three times Phantom Blade Zero's launch-day peak, and a record no 2026 release has approached. The gap matters because it disciplines the hype: Phantom Blade Zero is, by the day-one rankings, one of the biggest launches of 2026 (ahead of Resident Evil Requiem, Forza Horizon 6, and Crimson Desert's ~276,000 peak, per launch coverage), but it is not in Wukong's tier. Conflating "biggest of the year" with "Wukong-level" is the exact mistake launch-day coverage is designed to produce.

The honest caveats are all in the launch-day fine print, and they are the things to watch into the weekend:

- **Shader compilation stutter** was the most common player complaint, even among positive reviews — a PC-port tax that review scores mostly forgave and players experience every launch.
- **The story**, not the combat, is the critical knock — the reverse of the usual action-game complaint, and the thing most likely to cap word-of-mouth beyond the core audience.
- **A last-minute voice change**: trailers voiced Soul with Jay Maksenuk; the shipped game uses Roger Craig Smith (Sonic, Ezio, Chris Redfield), with IGN reporting it had asked both actors for comment. It is a footnote commercially, but it is the kind of late swap that fuels the "what changed and why" thread all weekend.
- **PS5 is a black box.** Sony publishes no concurrent counts, so nobody can say how big the total launch actually was — only that Steam alone was already enormous.

## The judgment

Judge this launch on Saturday, not Thursday. Launch-day peaks measure pre-launch marketing plus wishlist conversion; weekend peaks measure whether the people who bought it on Thursday told anyone on Friday. If Phantom Blade Zero holds near its peak into the weekend, the "second proof of a pipeline" reading is confirmed and the Wukong comparison becomes a question of legs, not launch. If it halves — the normal single-player decay once the day-one crowd finishes the opening hours — then the honest headline is narrower and still impressive: a $59.99 new IP from a Chinese studio opened in Steam's all-time top 20 for concurrent players, on combat alone. Either way, the door Wukong opened is now demonstrably a corridor, not a door that closed behind one game.
