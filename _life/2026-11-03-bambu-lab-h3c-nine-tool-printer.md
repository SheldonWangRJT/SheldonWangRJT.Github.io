## What launched today

Bambu Lab''s third-generation flagship is a tool changer, not another single-head printer with a filament switcher bolted on. The **H3C**, on sale from **November 3, 2026**, carries nine PrintTools in a retractable rack inside the chamber. Each tool has its own filament path, heater, cutter, and power/data connection, so a material change is a tool change — no purging a single nozzle through colour after colour, no purge tower eating filament and time.

The headline numbers, all from Bambu Lab:

| | H3C |
| --- | --- |
| Tools | 9 PrintTools, each 0.4 mm hardened steel nozzle, up to 350 °C |
| Tool change / material swap | 3.4 seconds / 6 seconds |
| Build volume | 330 × 320 × 325 mm |
| Toolhead speed / acceleration | 1,400 mm/s / 25,000 mm/s² |
| Max flow (ABS test) | 40 mm³/s |
| Price, AMS Combo (US) | **$2,499** before tax, shipping included |
| Price, AMS Combo (EU / UK) | €2,499 incl. VAT / £2,199 |

Every bundle ships with the new nine-slot **AMS 3H** built in, with active drying up to 85 °C on two of its nine bays. The **FlexFeed** extruder handles both standard 1.75 mm filament and thicker 2.85 mm filament, with a dedicated 2.85 mm path for soft TPU down to 75A — Bambu claims up to three times the flow rate of a classic printer on such material. Mixed nozzle sizes in a single print, and cutting and drawing tools swapped in without a manual change, are part of the same pitch. Bundles with filament, the Bambu Station base cabinet and the E1 air purifier run up to $3,049 in the US, and a rolling-cart bundle starts at $2,799 — a quiet admission that this is a tall, 36 kg-plus machine.

## The argument: purge towers were the tax; nine heaters are the bet

The reason multi-material printing stayed a compromise is simple: one nozzle serving many filaments must purge between each, wasting material and, worse, time. Bambu''s own earlier answer, the AMS filament switcher on the X1 and P1 series, made multi-colour printing reliable but never eliminated the purge. A tool changer attacks the root cause instead of managing it. If the 6-second swap holds in real prints, complex multi-material jobs — soluble supports plus engineering plastics, flexible hinges inside rigid parts, multi-colour gradients — stop carrying a purge penalty on every layer change.

That is also why the scepticism writes itself. Nine tools mean nine heaters, nine cutters, nine filament paths: nine of everything that can jam, misalign, or drift out of calibration. Bambu has not published a price for an individual replacement PrintTool, which matters precisely because there are nine of them. And the launch coverage is unusually honest about its own limits: early write-ups state plainly that they have not touched an H3C, and that everything so far comes from Bambu''s pages, press release, and store listing. The 6-second figure, the 40 mm³/s flow, the TPU claims — all manufacturer numbers, untested by anyone outside Bambu.

## The price comparison that decides it

The H3C does not launch into a vacuum. Its predecessor, the **H2C** — which swaps hotends rather than whole tools — is **$1,899 in Bambu''s Black Friday sale until December 1** (regular list higher depending on region). So the H3C asks roughly **$600 more** than the on-sale H2C for a genuinely different architecture, not a spec bump. Against the wider market, its named rival is Prusa''s CORE One INDX tool changer, and the H3C''s pitch is that it delivers the tool-changer workflow at a single integrated price, AMS included, rather than as an add-on ecosystem.

The judgment splits by buyer. If you run multi-material jobs for paying work — print farms, prototyping shops, anyone whose purge waste is a line item — the H3C is the first Bambu machine that removes the compromise rather than pricing around it, and the $600 premium over a sale-priced H2C is defensible. If you print multi-colour models for fun, the H2C at $1,899 is the better buy this month, and the H3C is a wait-for-reviews machine: let independent testers confirm the swap time, the calibration stability across nine tools, and the replacement-tool price before spending flagship money. Bambu earned its reputation by making the painful parts of 3D printing boring. The H3C asks to be judged on whether nine tools stay boring too — and that verdict needs someone other than Bambu holding the printer.
