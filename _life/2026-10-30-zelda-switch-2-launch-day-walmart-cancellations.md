*This is an update to this site's October 25 post, "A $519.99 Console With No Game in the Box Just Sold Out Worldwide." Prices below are US launch prices as reported October 29, 2026, a snapshot subject to change; restock status changes by the hour and should be rechecked before acting.*

## What happened on launch day

The Legend of Zelda 40th Anniversary Edition Switch 2 officially launched **Thursday, October 29**, at **$519.99** — hardware only, no game in the box. Nintendo met launch day with a restock on its own online store. It did not last: Polygon reported the store was "getting hammered under extreme demand," with stock going in and out of availability before showing out of stock again, and Game Rant's same-day update had the console sold out on Amazon by 11:55 AM ET. Major retailers — Amazon, Best Buy, Target, Walmart — were showing sold out.

The matching accessories launched alongside and sold out too: the Zelda Pro Controller at **$99.99** (a Nintendo Store exclusive version with a display stand at **$109.99**) and a carrying case with screen protector at **$39.99**.

## The Walmart cancellations

The ugliest launch-day fact is not the sellout — it is who got canceled. Starting Wednesday, the day before launch, Walmart customers who had pre-ordered the console received cancellation emails saying the item was out of stock, according to reports from Kotaku and Nintendo Life. Buyers who contacted support say they were told **Walmart oversold the console**. At the same time, third-party listings on Walmart's own site showed the console for around **$700**.

Walmart has not made a public statement, per the same reporting. And this is not a first offense: Walmart also canceled some original Switch 2 pre-orders around the June 2025 launch. A retailer that takes a pre-order, holds it for weeks, and cancels it the day before launch has not experienced scarcity — it has sold something it did not have.

## What is actually next

Nintendo's position has not changed since its Latin America chief Bill van Zyll admitted the company "underestimated" demand: replenishments are coming, no date attached. Two concrete windows exist:

- **Nintendo Store:** the listing reads "More inventory is on the way," with a wish-list alert in the Nintendo Store app.
- **Best Buy, November 5:** select stores plan to sell consoles at the midnight launch for *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* — the remake this console celebrates, which arrives November 5. Note the irony the launch-day coverage kept returning to: the console launched a week before the game it exists to celebrate.

Nintendo's New York and San Francisco stores sold launch-day units with a Warp Pipe Pass reservation, one console per customer.

## The judgment

There is a line in this story, and it runs between Nintendo and Walmart. Nintendo underestimated demand, said so on the record, and is restocking in public — that is scarcity, handled honestly, even if "please be patient" is cold comfort. Walmart took money-adjacent commitments it could not keep and canceled them at the last possible moment while its marketplace showed the same console at a ~$180 markup — that is a different category, and it is why the launch-day anger is aimed more at a retailer than at Nintendo.

For buyers, the October 25 advice stands, now with evidence: do not pay the scalper markup. The console is a cosmetic edition of a machine you can already buy, it contains no game, and the game that justifies it does not arrive until November 5 — by which point Best Buy's midnight window and Nintendo's promised replenishment will both have had a chance to land. The worst launch-day outcome is not missing the console. It is paying $700 for impatience, a week before the game comes out.
