*This is a follow-up to the October 21 preview on this site ("the arm-strain Mac Apple said it would never build"). Prices below are Apple's US configurator prices as queried October 29, 2026, and are a snapshot subject to change; hands-on impressions are from the first round of reviewer coverage, not independent testing.*

## What actually shipped

Apple opened pre-orders on **October 28, 2026** for the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display — its first touchscreen Mac — with availability from **November 6**. The headline specs are a tandem OLED panel with up to 2,000 nits peak brightness, 120Hz variable refresh, and the iPhone-style Dynamic Island moving to the Mac, on M5 Pro or M5 Max chips. Apple says a redesigned hinge and stabilization system keep the screen steady during touch input — its direct answer to the old Jobs objection that reaching for a vertical screen is "ergonomically terrible."

First, do not mix up the Macs. Apple announced three different machines in two days, and the naming invites exactly this confusion:

| Machine | Touchscreen? | Chip | US starting price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display (14"/16") | Yes, OLED touch | M5 Pro / M5 Max | $2,899 / $3,399 |
| MacBook Pro 14" (entry) | No | M6 | $1,999 |
| iMac 24" | No | M6 | $1,499 |

The $1,999 machine is not the touchscreen machine, and the touchscreen machine is not an M6 machine. The touch premium starts at $400 over the previous 14-inch M5 Pro configuration — and that is before memory and storage, where the real damage lives.

## The configurator is the story

Starting prices, per Apple and MacRumors' configurator reporting:

- 14-inch M5 Pro: **$2,899**; 16-inch M5 Pro: **$3,399**
- 14-inch M5 Max: **$4,499**; 16-inch M5 Max: **$4,799**
- Fully loaded 14-inch: **$14,249**; fully loaded 16-inch: **$14,549**

The top 16-inch machine carries the M5 Max with an 18-core CPU and 40-core GPU. The climb from $4,799 to $14,549 is mostly memory and storage: 128GB of memory adds about $2,000, 4TB of SSD adds $1,000 over the base, 8TB adds $3,000, and the new 16TB option adds $7,000. Nano-texture glass is $150 by comparison — a rounding error. The previous 16-inch MacBook Pro topped out at $10,149, so the new ceiling is **$4,400 higher**.

This is the memory crisis wearing a laptop's clothes. This site covered it twice this week: memory now eats half a cheap phone's bill of materials, and SK Hynix just said its output is effectively sold out through 2029. When a 16TB SSD upgrade costs $7,000, that is not Apple inventing a luxury tier out of thin air — it is a component market repricing what a pro machine can even be. Apple is passing the queue through to the buyer, with its margin intact.

## What the hands-ons actually say

The first hands-on roundup (Tom's Guide, The Verge, Engadget, CNBC, via the October 29 roundups) is unusually consistent, and it cuts against the launch framing:

- **Liked:** the display (Engadget calls it Apple's best yet), the lighter 3-pound-ish 14-inch design, the fingerprint-resistant coating, the glowing logo's return, the Dynamic Island.
- **Concerns:** price, no Apple Pencil support (confirmed by both Tom's Guide and The Verge), occasional touch stutter, screen wobble when tapped, fiddly small touch targets, and a display gain that CNBC says is "not night-and-day" over the previous panel.

That is the verdict in one line: touch on the Mac, in week one, is a pleasant extra for scrolling and tapping the occasional button — not a new input paradigm. Apple's own executives told WIRED the same thing in softer language: the Mac remains designed for keyboard and trackpad first; touch is an additional option. The generation raised on phones and tablets may eventually make it essential, but no reviewer this week claims it already is.

## The judgment

Buy this machine for the screen and the silicon, not for touch — and buy it configured, not aspirationally. The $2,899 starting price buys a genuinely new display on proven M5 silicon; the $14,549 price buys a memory-market hedge with a laptop attached. Anyone cross-shopping should price the exact configuration they need, because the configurator's slope between "reasonable pro machine" and "absurd" is now steeper than any single component justifies on its own — and it will stay steep until the memory queue shortens, which SK Hynix just told us is a 2029 conversation.

The taboo is broken, and that matters historically. But the more useful story from October 28 is narrower: Apple finally shipped the touchscreen Mac, priced it like the component crisis demands, and its own reviewers agree the touch itself is the least important thing about it. Wait for full reviews on battery life and sustained thermals before ordering the top end; the screen you can judge from a store visit, the $14,549 you cannot undo.

*Sources: Apple pre-order announcement and configurator prices via MacRumors (October 28–29, 2026); TechCrunch on the $14,549 ceiling and $10,149 prior ceiling; first hands-on roundup summarizing Tom's Guide, The Verge, Engadget, and CNBC; WIRED interview with Apple executives on touch as an addition, not a replacement.*
