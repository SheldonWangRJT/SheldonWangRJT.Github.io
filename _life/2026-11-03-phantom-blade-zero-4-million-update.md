*This is an update to our October 29 post on Phantom Blade Zero''s launch day. Sales figures are S-GAME''s own announcement; the studio gave no platform or regional split. Steam concurrent-player figures are SteamDB snapshots.*

## The weekend test, passed

Our launch-day post ended with a specific prediction: judge this game on Saturday, not Thursday. Launch-day peaks measure wishlist conversion; weekend peaks measure whether Thursday''s buyers told anyone on Friday. Phantom Blade Zero passed that test in the most legible way possible — its peak did not decay into the weekend, it climbed. From a reported launch-day peak of about 705,643, SteamDB recorded **833,921 concurrent players on Sunday, November 1**.

The numbers S-GAME announced on **November 2**:

| | Phantom Blade Zero | Black Myth: Wukong (2024) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copies in first 72 hours | **Over 4 million** | Over 10 million |
| Steam peak concurrent players | **833,921** (Nov 1) | Over 2.4 million |
| Pace | ~15.4 copies per second for three days | — |
| Steam all-time rank by peak | **14th highest** | Far higher |

The game also became, by the same coverage, the biggest Steam launch of 2026 so far — ahead of Forza Horizon 6 and every other premium release this year — while holding a Very Positive Steam rating (about 89% positive in launch-weekend coverage) and a 9/10 from IGN. Pre-launch demand had already signalled this: over 3 million wishlists across PS5 and PC in September, and roughly 1 million copies sold before launch across Steam, PS5, and Tencent''s China-only WeGame store, per pre-launch estimates.

## What 4 million does and does not prove

What it proves: the pipeline reading from launch day now has a second leg. A full-price, single-player, wuxia action game with no live-service hooks — you play Soul, an assassin with 66 days to live — topped Steam''s revenue chart and held its audience through the weekend. That is a commercial result most Western AAA launches never reach, from a studio, S-GAME, that spent most of its life making mobile games. The "second proof" argument is no longer a launch-day inference; it is a first-week fact.

What it does not prove is the comparison everyone wants to make. Four million is 40% of Wukong''s three-day total, and 833,921 is about a third of Wukong''s peak. The honest framing is not "the next Wukong" but "the second data point after Wukong" — a distinction that matters because it changes what publishers should copy. Wukong was a cultural event in China first and a global hit second; Phantom Blade Zero''s pre-launch sales were likewise heavily Greater-China-weighted, and S-GAME has not published a regional split for the 4 million. Some coverage notes the figure may include WeGame copies; the studio has not confirmed the storefront breakdown. Treat the 4 million as a real, studio-announced total with an unpublished composition, not as a like-for-like Steam-plus-PS5 count.

The caveats carried over from launch week, and they are now the things to watch in week two. S-GAME acknowledged further bug fixes are needed, and launch coverage flagged performance complaints plus a warning that NVIDIA''s 621.01 driver was causing instability, with a rollback recommended. Single-player sales curves also fall steeply after week one by nature; the question is no longer whether the game opened big, but how far it falls and how fast — and whether post-launch support turns a great opening into a long tail. PS5 remains a black box, as Sony publishes no concurrent counts.

## The judgment

The weekend answered the question launch day posed, and the answer is better than the cautious version: this was not a one-day wishlist burn. It was a genuine breakout that grew into Sunday. But the Wukong comparison, held to the numbers rather than the excitement, still says "not yet, and not close" — 4 million against 10, a third of the peak. That is not a disappointment; it is the correct scale. One game proved a Chinese single-player action title could be a global event. The second proved the first was not a fluke. A third will decide whether this is an industry shift or a two-game run — and that is a much more interesting question than another round of "is it Wukong yet?"
