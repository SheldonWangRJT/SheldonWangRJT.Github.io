*All figures below are from Intel's Q3 2026 earnings release and call on October 29, 2026, with analyst expectations via Reuters/LSEG as reported by CNBC and others. Share-price moves are snapshots from Thursday after-hours and Friday premarket trading and vary by report; they are not a closing price.*

## The beat that did not matter

Start with the quarter itself, because it was genuinely good. Intel reported Q3 2026 revenue of **$16.51 billion, up 21% year over year**, and adjusted EPS of **$0.52 against a roughly $0.40 consensus** — its eighth consecutive quarter of beating its own guidance. Data Center and AI revenue hit **$7.3 billion, up 78% year over year**, the segment's strongest growth in 15 years. Adjusted gross margin was **44.1%**, above the 42% guided.

Then the stock fell off a cliff: about **23% in Thursday after-hours trading**, and around **25% in Friday premarket trading** per CNBC data — one of the worst single-session reactions in the company's history. The reason was not the quarter. It was everything after it.

## The guide: $1.6 billion short, and the reasons are all physical

For Q4, Intel guided to revenue of **$15–16 billion** (midpoint $15.5 billion) against a **$17.07 billion** consensus, adjusted EPS of **$0.25 against $0.43 expected**, and gross margin of **38%** against 44.1% in Q3. Three separate physical problems stack up inside that guide:

**1. A fab explosion.** An October 14 blast at Fab 28 in Kiryat Gat, Israel, killed two workers and seriously injured a third, and shut the fab for about two weeks. CFO David Zinsner put the cost at roughly **$300 million of lost Q4 revenue plus 200 basis points of gross margin**, almost all of it tied to 10-nanometer (Intel 7) server products, with a similar ~$300 million revenue hit expected in Q1 2027 — about **$600 million across two quarters** in total. Most wafer write-offs land in Q4. Production was expected to restart by the end of that week, but Intel is still assessing which wafers in the line can be salvaged.

**2. A PC market falling apart under memory prices.** Client Computing revenue already fell 5% in Q3 to $8.1 billion, and Intel expects it to drop **more than 20% sequentially in Q4** as OEMs slash inventory. Zinsner expects industry PC units to decline about **12% in 2026**, with second-half units down more than 20%, and fall roughly **10% more in 2027**. CEO Lip-Bu Tan's budget chip line, Wildcat Lake, is reportedly **sold out** — demand "outstripping supply" — which sounds like good news until you realize it means Intel is selling every cheap chip it can make into a market that is shrinking anyway, while management says it is "not optimistic" about memory supply improving in 2027.

**3. Server demand it cannot meet.** The cruel mirror image: data-center demand is there, but supply is not. Intel says server CPU supply will remain constrained **through 2027 and into 2028**, citing Intel 3 cycle times, advanced substrate shortages, and the Fab 28 shutdown. It is adding Intel 3 production in Oregon, but says substrate capacity additions will matter more in the second half of 2027. Meanwhile, the data-center growth that drove Q3 — Granite Rapids, now Intel's largest server family — is exactly the business Fab 28's outage hits.

## The judgment

This is the worst kind of earnings reaction because it is not about one bad number — it is about a company caught between two shortages moving in opposite directions. On the server side, demand exceeds what Intel can physically build. On the PC side, demand is evaporating because memory prices have pushed system prices up, and Intel's own forecast says that does not get better next year. A fab explosion that takes out older-node server supply is the worst possible accident at the worst possible moment: it hits the one segment that was working.

The turnaround narrative survived eight straight guidance beats. What it could not survive is a guide that says, in effect: we cannot make enough of what people want, and people increasingly do not want what we can make. Until server supply loosens — management's own timeline says late 2027 at the earliest — Intel's stock is a bet on fab execution, not on demand. And fab execution just had its worst week in years.
