## What Sony is offering

On November 2, 2026, Sony began sending US PlayStation owners a notification titled "Trade in your PS5 console for up to $400." The offer is handled by Back Market, the refurbished-electronics marketplace founded in Paris in 2014: owners get a quote online, ship the console, and receive a cash payment to their bank account — not PlayStation Store credit — after inspection. The notification was first surfaced by a screenshot shared on the ResetEra forum, and was reported on November 3–4 by Polygon, Kotaku, TweakTown, Hot Hardware, and Dexerto.

The timing is not subtle. Grand Theft Auto 6 launches on November 19, and Sony clearly wants the upgrade conversation happening now, while players are deciding which hardware they will play it on.

## The fine print behind the $400

The headline number is doing a lot of work. According to Sony's own notification, the $400 is the average quote for a standard PS5 in "Grade 0" — effectively flawless — condition, bundled with two or more controllers, based on trade-in orders created between August 23 and September 22, 2026. A worn console, a single controller, or a Digital Edition all start lower, and Back Market inspects every unit before confirming the final price.

The live quotes tell the same story. Back Market's own trade-in pages put a flawless standard PS5 at up to $415, a Digital Edition at $385, and a PS5 Pro at $725 as of November 3. Hot Hardware tested the tool and found a standard PS5 in great condition with just one controller quoted at $408. Meanwhile, Kotaku notes that used PS5s are currently selling for around $435 on eBay — meaning a private sale can beat Sony's headline offer before the two-controller requirement even enters the picture.

## The upgrade the trade-in is supposed to fund does not exist on shelves

Here is the part that makes the campaign read oddly. The PS5 Pro now costs $899.99 in the US, up from its $699 launch price, and it has been sold out since September 19, 2026 at Best Buy, Amazon, Walmart, Target, and PlayStation Direct — the five channels that cover nearly all US sales. Sony has told distributors the shortage will last "at least into early 2027." A best-case $400 trade-in would cut the Pro upgrade to about $500, but only for a buyer who can actually find a Pro to buy. Today, that buyer largely cannot.

The resale market has noticed. Kotaku reports GameStop is currently selling used PS5 Pros for up to $1,400 — more than 55% above the already-raised retail price. And Sony's own pricing history undercuts the generosity of the offer: the all-digital PS5 launched at $400 in 2020, and its official price has since risen to $600. Sony is offering, at most, the 2020 launch price of a digital PS5 for a used standard model in 2026.

## The judgment

There is a real service buried in here: a no-hassle, cash-paid trade-in for a console gathering dust is genuinely useful for someone who has moved on from PlayStation. But as an upgrade pitch timed to GTA 6, the campaign promises a path that Sony's own supply chain cannot deliver. The company raised the Pro to $899.99, cannot keep it in stock, and is now offering to buy back the cheaper console at a price a private sale beats — while the used Pros that do exist sell for $1,400. If your PS5 is genuinely unused, take the quote and compare it with eBay before shipping anything. If you were hoping this was your ticket to a Pro before November 19, the trade-in was never the bottleneck. The shelf was.
