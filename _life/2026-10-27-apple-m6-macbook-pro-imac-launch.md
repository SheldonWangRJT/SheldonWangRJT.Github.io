## What Apple announced today

On **Tuesday, October 27, 2026**, Apple unveiled two M6 machines (Reuters, MacRumors, 9to5Mac):

**14-inch MacBook Pro with M6 — from $1,999** ($1,899 with education pricing)

- **12-core CPU and 12-core GPU**, up to **30% faster graphics** than the M5 model (Apple via Reuters/9to5Mac).
- **Dual 16-core Neural Engine**: 9to5Mac reports up to **1.9× faster** than M5 on neural workloads and up to **1.3× faster time-to-first-token** for LLM prompt processing versus the M5 14-inch model; Reuters summarised Apple's AI claim as up to **25% faster AI performance**.
- **Up to 24 hours of battery life** — Apple calls it the longest ever in a Mac — with fast charging to 50% in 30 minutes.
- **16GB unified memory and 1TB storage as standard** (MacRumors notes the 512GB tier was already removed in March), configurable to 32GB and 4TB; memory bandwidth up to 170GB/s; **Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6**; Space Black and Silver.

**iMac with M6 — from $1,499**

- A bigger generational jump: the iMac was last refreshed with **M4 in 2024**, and Apple claims up to **5× faster on-device AI** than that model.
- 24-inch 4.5K Retina display; 10-core CPU/GPU at $1,499, 12-core at **$1,699**; dual Neural Engine up to 2× the previous generation's AI compute; **2.5Gb Ethernet**; Wi-Fi 7 / Bluetooth 6; seven colours.

Both open for **pre-order on Wednesday, October 28** in 35 countries and regions, with availability **Friday, November 6**.

## The elephant arriving tomorrow

None of this is the week's headline Mac. Tomorrow, **October 28**, Apple hosts its **"Pro and Tell"** event in Los Angeles, where Bloomberg has reported Apple will unveil its first **touchscreen OLED MacBook Pro** — the redesign this site covered on October 21: touch-enabled OLED, lighter body, Dynamic Island-style cutout replacing the notch, and, per Bloomberg reporting, **M5 Pro / M5 Max** chips rather than M6.

So Apple is asking for M6 pre-orders on the exact morning the touch model becomes real. Two readings:

**The charitable one:** these are different products for different buyers. The M6 14-inch is the efficient, longest-battery, no-touch workhorse; the touch OLED model is the expensive redesign. Pocket-lint made the structural point: today's M6 refresh is deliberately limited to the 14-inch — no 16-inch — because the redesign owns the top of the range tomorrow.

**The colder one:** every pre-order taken tomorrow morning is taken from a buyer who has not yet seen the touch model's price. If the OLED touch Pro lands close to $1,999, today's machine is instantly the awkward middle. If it lands far above, today's launch looks like a value anchor planted 24 hours early. Either way, Apple chose the sequence.

## The verdict

Do not pre-order tomorrow morning. That is the whole advice, and it costs you nothing: availability is November 6 either way, and waiting one day converts a guess into a comparison.

On the merits, the M6 14-inch is a strong, boring update in the best sense — the standard 1TB alone quietly fixes the stingiest part of the old base configuration, 24 hours is a real threshold for a Pro laptop, and the iMac's M4-to-M6 leap (with 5× on-device AI and 2.5Gb Ethernet) is the cleaner buy of the two for anyone on an M1 or M4 all-in-one.

But "strong update" and "buy it on day one of pre-orders" stopped being the same sentence the moment Apple scheduled a touchscreen redesign reveal for the following day. The M6 Pro's real competitor is not the M5 it replaces. It is the machine Apple shows tomorrow — and Apple knows it, which is why you are reading this launch today.
