*This is an update to [our October 21 roundup of 24 iPhone Duo reviews](/life/iphone-duo-review-roundup/). Prices are US retail as of October 23, 2026 — a snapshot, subject to change. The production-target figure below is supply-chain reporting (DigiTimes, October 22) and is explicitly unconfirmed by Apple. Apple has published no unit sales or production numbers.*

## One minute, five minutes, January

The launch-day scoreboard, by market:

- **South Korea:** the first pre-order batch at SK Telecom's T Direct Shop sold out in **one minute**; KT's first and second batches of the 256GB and 512GB models went in the same timeframe. About 100 people queued before opening at Apple's Myeongdong store in Seoul, and walk-in customers without reservations could not buy at all (Aju Press).
- **India:** Blinkit launched the Duo in Delhi-NCR at 8:15 a.m. and its **entire inventory was gone within five minutes**, per CEO Albinder Dhindsa's own post on X.
- **United States:** overnight lines at stores from Fifth Avenue to Las Vegas — one shopper counted a queue growing from 30–40 people at 4:15 a.m. to more than 150 by morning; a Delaware store drew an estimated 200–300. Orlando's Mall at Millenia stopped accepting walk-ins by about **8:15 a.m.**; a Boise store sold out after roughly its first 50–60 customers (New York Post, compiling on-the-ground and social reports).
- **Online:** delivery estimates for new orders sit at **January 25, 2027** — where they have been since roughly 30 minutes after pre-orders opened on October 16 (MacRumors). Apple told USA Today stores would receive a "limited" supply for walk-ins and expected it to sell out quickly. It did.

And the secondary market priced the shortage before launch day even arrived: confirmed pre-orders resold on eBay for up to **$4,999.99** — a 2TB model at an ~$1,800 premium over its $3,199 retail, with 256GB units (retail $1,999) changing hands at $2,699–$3,800 (MacRumors / Dexerto sold listings, October 16).

## The argument: demand, or a shortage doing marketing's job?

Two readings of the same day are fighting across social media, and launch day genuinely supports both.

**The demand reading.** People queued overnight, in multiple countries, for a $1,999 first-generation product whose reviews — all 24 of them — said the apps are not ready and Face ID is gone. That is not spec-sheet shopping; that is pull. The reported supply response points the same way: DigiTimes reported on October 22 that Apple had asked Foxconn and component suppliers to raise the Duo's 12-month shipment target by **44%, from 9.7 million to 14 million units** — unconfirmed, but not the move you make for a product that is merely scarce on purpose. JPMorgan's expectation of 8–9 million units sold in 2026 sits well below that target, which suggests Apple is either seeing something analysts are not, or stocking for 2027 demand it expects to persist.

**The scarcity reading.** Apple never published a single unit number, capped purchases at two per customer, and let its own retail staff warn customers away from walk-in attempts — a shortage announced in advance is also a launch strategy. And the map was not uniformly desperate: MacRumors staff bought a Duo in Edinburgh with no difficulty, at a store that received *more* units than expected, and at Manhattan Beach roughly 50 people queued and *everyone* got a phone. A truly demand-overwhelmed launch does not have quiet stores; a tightly allocated one does, wherever allocation guesses wrong.

## My call

Both are true, and the proportions matter less than the mechanism. A first-generation foldable with a new hinge, a new display stack and a 5.2mm unfolded chassis was never going to ship in iPhone-18 volumes — the supply ceiling is real engineering, not theatre. What Apple chose is what it *did with* that ceiling: launch anyway, at full price, into a January backlog, rather than delay until stock was comfortable. That choice converts a manufacturing constraint into three months of sold-out headlines — and, judging by the overnight lines for a phone whose own reviewers scored it 7/10 at The Verge and WIRED, the conversion worked.

The number to watch is not the sellout; it is the **January 25 date**. If it holds or slips further while the reported 44% production increase works through, the demand story wins outright. If delivery snaps back to days within a few weeks, then the shortage was shallower than the launch made it look — and the lines were buying a story as much as a phone.
