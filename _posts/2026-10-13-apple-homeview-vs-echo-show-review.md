## TL;DR

Apple's HomeView is the nicer object and the more private one. Amazon's Echo Show is the better video screen, the cheaper buy, and the more open smart home hub. If your kitchen routine is "play a YouTube recipe video while I cook," buy the Echo Show. If your household lives in Apple Home, hates ads on a screen, and wants FaceTime in the kitchen, the HomeView is worth its $299 — with one big asterisk: **Siri AI's full tier will eventually cost a fee Apple hasn't priced yet.**

## What Apple actually announced

At its "Welcome Home" event on October 13, 2026, Apple introduced HomeView, its first smart display. Pre-orders opened the same day; both models ship **November 13** for **$299**. Launch markets are the US, UK, Canada, Australia, India, and New Zealand (Singapore pre-orders October 15, South Africa via resellers). **It is not sold in the EU**, because Siri AI is currently unavailable in iOS there.

There are two models at the same price:

- **HomeView (tabletop):** 7-inch display on a domed aluminum speaker base, joined by a stainless steel tilt hinge. Four colors: silver, starlight, rose pink, space gray, each with a color-matched USB-C cable. In the box: 1.5 m USB-C cable + 30W adapter.
- **HomeView (Wall Mounted):** same display and features in silver and space gray, sold screen-only. The wall mount is **not included** — three Schneider Home mounts sold separately: Power Mount USB-C Wall Connector (from $59.95), Power Mount AC Wired (from $79.95), Power Mount PoE Wired ($129.95, early 2027). So the real wall-mounted cost is **$358.95 minimum**.

## Design and screen: square vs rectangle

HomeView's defining choice is its **7-inch square** Liquid Retina display (1,088 × 1,088, per Pocket-lint). It looks like a giant Apple Watch face — Apple leans into that with swipeable "faces" (Photos, Flow, Snoopy, Home Modular, Home Camera), and the interface shows simpler info from across the room and richer detail as you step closer. Brightness runs from 500 nits down to **0.3 nits**, so it can sit on a nightstand without lighting up the room.

The Echo Show 8 (4th gen) is a **8.7-inch rectangle** (1,340 × 800) — basically a small tablet on a fabric-mesh base. It looks less designed, but the shape matters more than the looks:

**The letterbox math.** A 16:9 YouTube video on HomeView's 1,088 × 1,088 square renders at 1,088 × 612 — roughly **44% of the screen is black bars**. On the Echo Show 8's 1,340 × 800 panel, the same video renders at ~1,340 × 754, with only ~6% black bars. Apple's square is a smart-home dashboard shape; Amazon's rectangle is a video shape. If you watch a lot of video in the kitchen, this alone is a reason to pick the Show.

## Siri AI vs Alexa+: read the subscription fine print

Both devices are AI-assistant plays, and both have a subscription asterisk — but the asterisks are shaped differently.

**Siri AI (HomeView).** An entirely new Siri, powered by Apple Intelligence. HomeView recognizes up to **six people by face** and gives each a personalized Home Screen, apps, and widgets. Personal content (Messages, Calendar) only surfaces when the enrolled user is recognized *and* their paired iPhone/iPad is nearby and recently unlocked. With an Apple Intelligence iPhone/iPad, Siri AI draws on personal context across apps; without one, you still get broad world knowledge and natural conversation. Conversations sync via iCloud to the Siri app on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch, and Vision Pro.

The catch, in Apple's own footnote: Siri AI is in beta, "certain Apple Intelligence features that rely on server-side models are subject to **daily usage limits**," and "**expanded access to such features will be available for a fee in the future**." Apple has not named the price. So HomeView's $299 buys the hardware plus an unknown future AI bill. Setup requires a compatible iPhone or iPad, and the full personal-context experience needs an Apple Intelligence device.

**Alexa+ (Echo Show).** Amazon's LLM-powered Alexa, free with Prime ($139/year), or **$19.99/month** standalone. The price is known, which makes the math honest — and it cuts both ways (see the 3-year table below). Alexa+ is a known quantity in a way Siri AI isn't yet: it's been in real homes, with real reviews calling it solid but still a work in progress. Siri AI on HomeView is a launch-day promise; Alexa+ is a shipping product.

## Smart home: Apple Home vs Alexa's everything

HomeView is a full Apple Home hub: Matter controller + Thread Border Router, controlling lights, locks, thermostats, blinds, and cameras by touch or voice. Its Home Camera face livestreams **up to nine camera feeds at once**. With an eligible iCloud+ plan, it adds intelligent recognition (people, familiar faces, pets, packages, vehicles) and AI video descriptions for HomeKit Secure Video clips. It also detects smoke/CO alarm sounds and alerts your iPhone/Watch.

Apple also launched **Apple Home Collection** — cameras, thermostats, and a lock designed with LG and Schneider Electric — arriving December, with select accessories in early 2027.

The Echo Show's smart home story is broader but shallower per-device: Alexa controls an enormous catalog, including Ring cameras and doorbells (Amazon-owned), with routines and automations. What it doesn't have is Apple's per-person privacy model or the HomeKit Secure Video encryption story. What HomeView doesn't have is Ring: WSJ's first impressions note you can't use third-party smart home apps on HomeView — a Ring camera or a non-HomeKit garage opener won't show up. **If your house runs on Ring, HomeView is a non-starter as your main dashboard.**

## YouTube and video: the detail that decides it

This is the section that matters most in practice, so here it is in full.

### HomeView: no YouTube app, no App Store, no Netflix — Safari or AirPlay only

HomeView has **no App Store**. Apple ships a curated set of apps (FaceTime, Messages, Safari, Music, Podcasts, News) plus a handful of third-party media apps like Spotify, and developers can bring existing **CarPlay-framework** apps over — if your iPhone already has one installed, it can appear on HomeView after setup.

**There is no native YouTube, Netflix, or Disney+ app.** Wired's hands-on confirms video goes through Safari, "which is not the best experience." And it's worse than that: even Apple's *own* video apps don't run natively. Per AppleInsider's analysis of Apple's own materials, **Apple TV, Apple Music Classical, and Apple Books audiobooks must be AirPlayed from your iPhone** to the HomeView (Apple News+ may be Siri-read-only). Apple's marketing says HomeView is "the perfect way to enjoy… TV shows," but the fine print routes TV through your phone.

What this means in the kitchen:

1. **"Play this YouTube recipe"** = open Safari, navigate to youtube.com, fight touch controls in a browser — or AirPlay from your iPhone, which defeats the point of a hands-free kitchen screen.
2. **Square screen** = 44% black bars on every 16:9 video (see the letterbox math above).
3. **The fix is hypothetical:** YouTube, Netflix, and Disney+ *could* appear if those companies adopt the CarPlay framework. None has announced that. Buying HomeView for video today means betting on someone else's roadmap.

### Echo Show: no native YouTube either — but the workaround is one voice command

The Echo Show 8/11 also lack a native YouTube app (the exception is the Show 15's Fire TV flavor). But the workaround is far more baked in:

- Say **"Alexa, open YouTube"** → the Silk (or Firefox) browser opens youtube.com. You can sign in and get subscriptions, playlists, and history.
- Voice control inside the browser is partial: **pause, play, and volume work; skip, next, and in-page search don't.** You still tap for most navigation.
- Unlike HomeView, the Show has **native Netflix, Hulu, and Prime Video apps** — so YouTube is the exception, not the rule, on video.
- The 16:9-ish rectangle fits video nearly edge to edge.

The browser path is still a workaround, and the Google–Amazon history means it's not guaranteed forever. But as of today, "Alexa, open YouTube" is a one-command habit; HomeView's equivalent is a multi-step fumble.

**Bottom line on video:** if YouTube-in-the-kitchen is a top-3 use case, the Echo Show wins this category outright. HomeView buyers are choosing the screen *despite* its video story, not because of it.

## Privacy and ads: Apple's strongest card

This is where HomeView genuinely pulls away.

- **Secure Exclaves:** camera and microphone data are processed in protected hardware runtimes that HomeView software, apps, users, and even Apple cannot access.
- **Face-gated personal content:** your Messages and Calendar only appear when *you* are recognized and your unlocked iPhone is nearby. Kids and guests can't snoop.
- **No ads.** Apple's business model here is hardware + (eventually) an AI fee, not attention.
- **One hard privacy limit:** you **cannot** view a live feed from HomeView's own built-in camera — it can't double as an indoor security camera the way an Echo Show or Nest Hub can (WSJ). And there's **no physical camera shutter**, so you're trusting software + the Secure Exclave story.

The Echo Show, by contrast, is an ad surface. WSJ's Nicole Nguyen, who uses one daily, writes that she's "sick of the screen showing me ads" — and that getting it to display her photos instead costs extra. Amazon's model subsidizes cheaper hardware with attention and subscriptions. If a screen in your home showing ads bothers you, that alone may justify HomeView's premium.

## The money: 3-year total cost

Sticker prices lie by omission on both sides. Here's my own arithmetic — list prices, no tax, no discounts, 36 months:

| Setup | Hardware | AI / subscription (36 mo) | 3-year total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HomeView (tabletop) | $299 | **Unknown** — Siri AI fee unpriced | **$299 + ?** |
| HomeView (wall) | $299 + $59.95 mount | Unknown | **$358.95 + ?** |
| Echo Show 8 + Prime | $199.99 | Alexa+ included in Prime* | **$199.99** |
| Echo Show 8, no Prime | $199.99 | Alexa+ $19.99 × 36 = $719.64 | **$919.63** |
| Echo Show 11 + Prime | $249.99 | Alexa+ included in Prime* | **$249.99** |
| Echo Show 11, no Prime | $249.99 | Alexa+ $19.99 × 36 = $719.64 | **$969.63** |

\*Prime itself is $139/year, but it buys far more than Alexa+ (shipping, Prime Video, etc.), so I don't bill it to the display. If you'd *only* buy Prime for Alexa+, add $417 over 3 years.

Read that table carefully: **the Echo Show is only cheap if you're already a Prime member.** Without Prime, the standalone Alexa+ bill makes the Show *triple* the HomeView's hardware price over three years — and Apple hasn't priced its fee yet, so HomeView's row has an open variable. The honest summary: Prime members get the best deal in this category; everyone else is choosing between two unpriced/pricey AI futures.

Also remember street prices: Echo Shows go on sale constantly (Show 8 hit $125, Show 11 hit $150 in recent Prime events). HomeView won't see discounts until well after launch. If you can wait for Black Friday, the Show's effective price is far below list.

## Verdict: who should buy which

**Buy the HomeView if:** your household runs on Apple Home / Matter; you want FaceTime, Messages, and Find My on a shared screen; you want six-person face personalization with real privacy gating; ads on a home screen bother you; and you accept that video means AirPlay/Safari for now, plus an unknown future Siri AI fee.

**Buy the Echo Show 8 if:** you want the best value (especially as a Prime member); YouTube-in-the-kitchen matters; your house has Zigbee gear or Ring cameras; you want native Netflix/Hulu/Prime Video; and you can live with ads and Amazon's data model.

**Buy the Echo Show 11 if:** you want the same as the Show 8 but with an 11-inch 1080p screen for $50 more at list — and you don't need a physical camera shutter (the 11 dropped it, unlike the Show 15).

**Wait if:** you're in the EU (HomeView isn't sold there yet); you want to see what Apple's Siri AI fee actually is; or you want a HomeView with a YouTube app — which requires Google to adopt the CarPlay framework, and nobody has announced that.

My own read: HomeView is a beautiful, privacy-first Gen-1 product with a real video hole and an unpriced AI future. The Echo Show is the boring, practical, ad-supported answer that already does the kitchen-video job. Apple is betting the home screen is about *who's looking at it*; Amazon is betting it's about *what's playing on it*. For most kitchens in 2026, what's playing still wins.
